🎙️ Recording level CLIP

-60-30-90 dBFS

For recording, keep the loudest parts in the green (around −18 to −6 dBFS). Red means clipping, which distorts.

🎯 Calibrate (make the numbers match a real meter)

Phone and computer microphones differ, so the reading is an estimate that can be several decibels off. Hold your device next to a real sound level meter (or a calibrator at 94 dB), and type what it shows while the sound plays.

Browsers can still change the microphone volume on some phones. For the steadiest reading, use the same device each time and keep it about an arm’s length from the instrument, where your ears are.

How to tune your instrument

🎸 Guitar, bass and ukulele
  1. Pick your instrument and tuning, then press Start microphone.
  2. Pluck one string firmly and let it ring. The tuner shows which string it hears.
  3. Needle on the left (flat): tighten the string. On the right (sharp): loosen it.
  4. Stop when the needle is in the green middle and it says In tune. Then do the next string.
  5. Go through all strings twice: tuning one string slightly changes the tension on the others.
⬆️ Always tune up to the note

If a string is sharp, go a little below the note and come back up. That way the string settles against the peg and stays in tune longer. New strings stretch for a few days, so they need tuning often.

🎻 Violin, viola, cello and double bass

Tune the A string first, usually with the peg, then the others with the fine tuners. Bow gently in the middle of the string — a steady, quiet sound gives the steadiest reading. Many orchestras tune to A = 442 Hz; set it above.

🎺 Wind instruments and voice

Choose Chromatic. Play a long, steady note at a normal volume. Trumpet, clarinet and tenor sax players can switch the note names to B♭, alto sax to E♭ and horn to F, so the tuner shows the note on your sheet music. Warm up first: a cold instrument plays flat.

🎚️ Alternate tunings

Drop D lowers the lowest guitar string to D for heavy riffs. DADGAD and the open tunings make a chord when you strum the open strings — great for slide and folk. “Half step down” matches many rock records. The tuner’s target notes change with the tuning you pick.

🔢 What are cents?

A cent is a hundredth of a semitone (the step between two piano keys). Most people cannot hear a difference below about 5 cents, which is why the green zone is ±5 cents. A4 = 440 Hz is the international standard; baroque groups often use 415 Hz.

How loud is too loud?

Hearing damage depends on how loud and how long. Following the US NIOSH recommendation, 85 dB(A) is safe for 8 hours a day, and every 3 dB more halves the safe time. Damage is permanent and builds up silently — ringing ears or muffled hearing after playing are warning signs.

Practising with a metronome

🐢 Slow first

Play the hard part slowly enough to get it right every time, then add a few BPM. The speed trainer does this for you: for example +5 BPM every 4 bars up to 140.

🤫 Keep time without help

The gap trainer plays a few bars, then goes silent. If you are still on the beat when it comes back, your inner clock is good. Start with 2 on, 2 off.

🎶 Subdivide

Clicking eighths, triplets or sixteenths makes fast passages even. Swing eighths give the long-short feel of jazz and blues.

🥁 Odd time signatures

7/8 is grouped 2+2+3 and 5/8 as 3+2 here — press the beats to move the accents to the grouping your piece uses.

About Tune Forge

Tune Forge is a free online tuner for guitar, bass, ukulele, violin, viola, cello, double bass, mandolin, banjo, balalaika and any other instrument or voice, with a metronome, a sound level meter for musicians, reference tones and a drone.

How to use Tune Forge

  1. Press “Start microphone”, pick your instrument and tuning, and play one string at a time until the needle is in the green.
  2. Open “Metronome” to practise in time, with tap tempo, a speed trainer and a gap trainer.
  3. Open “Sound level” to see how loud your playing is, how long it is safe for your ears, and whether your recording level clips.

How do I tune my guitar with this online tuner?

Press Start microphone and allow the microphone. Pick Guitar and the tuning, such as Standard (E A D G B E). Pluck one string and let it ring: the tuner finds the string and the needle shows if it is flat (left) or sharp (right). Turn the tuning peg until the needle is in the green middle and the screen says In tune.

Is the decibel meter accurate?

It is a good estimate, not a certified sound level meter. Phone and laptop microphones differ, so readings can be off by several decibels. For better accuracy, set the calibration once using a real sound level meter or a known sound.

How loud is too loud for my ears?

NIOSH recommends at most 8 hours a day at 85 dB(A). Every 3 dB more halves the safe time: 88 dB is 4 hours, 91 dB is 2 hours, 100 dB only 15 minutes.

Should I tune to A = 440 Hz or 432 Hz?

440 Hz is the international standard and what other players, pianos and recordings use. Some baroque groups use 415 Hz and some orchestras 442 or 443 Hz. 432 Hz is a personal preference; there is no scientific evidence that it sounds healthier.

Is Tune Forge free and private?

Yes. It is free with no account. The microphone sound is analysed on your device and is never recorded or uploaded.
