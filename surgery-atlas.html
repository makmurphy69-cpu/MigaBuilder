Surgery Atlas

How operations are done and how surgery began: why each operation is needed, the main steps, recovery and risks, and the people who first did it. From ancient skull drilling to robots.

About Surgery Atlas

Surgery Atlas explains 124 operations and milestones of surgery across six eras, from ancient Egypt, India, Greece and China to anaesthesia and antisepsis, the first heart operations and transplants, and today’s keyhole, laser and robotic surgery. Each operation has a simple illustration of where it is done, why it is needed, the main steps, recovery, the main risks and its history — including the patients, nurses and technicians whose part was long forgotten.

How to use Surgery Atlas

  1. Search an operation or a body part, or pick a group such as Heart & blood vessels or Transplants.
  2. Open a card to see why it is done, how it is done step by step, recovery, risks and its history.
  3. Use the timeline to follow surgery from ancient times to robots.
  4. Play the quiz, or download a printable quiz with answers for a class.

Is Surgery Atlas medical advice?

No. It explains operations in general terms, like a hospital information leaflet, for learning. Every patient is different — ask your own doctor or surgeon about your care.

Are the pictures graphic?

No. Each operation is shown with a simple drawing of the body, the organ involved and the kind of instrument used — nothing gory.

Is Surgery Atlas free?

Yes. It is free to use, with no account and no paywall. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded.
