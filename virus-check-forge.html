What this can and can’t do. It catches the tricks most email and download malware relies on: programs pretending to be documents, macros, scripts, booby-trapped PDFs and shortcuts. It does not have a full virus database, so brand-new malware inside an ordinary-looking program can pass. “No warning signs” does not prove a file is safe. For a second opinion, look the fingerprint up on VirusTotal, and keep your computer’s own antivirus switched on.

Think your computer or phone has a virus?

No website can scan or clean your computer. Browsers don’t let web pages see your files or programs. If a page suddenly says your device is infected, shows a phone number, or wants you to download a “cleaner”, it is a scam: close the tab (or the whole browser), and never call the number.

Real removal needs software installed on the device. Most devices already have it. Open a section for the steps:

🪟 Windows
  1. Open Windows Security → Virus & threat protection → Scan options, choose Full scan and press Scan now.
  2. Then run Microsoft Defender Offline scan from the same list. The PC restarts and removes malware that hides while Windows runs.
  3. For a second opinion, use the free Malwarebytes or Microsoft Safety Scanner, downloaded only from their official sites.
  4. In Settings → Apps, uninstall programs you don’t recognise, and remove unknown browser extensions.
🍎 Mac
  1. Install the latest macOS update — its built-in protection (XProtect) removes known malware automatically.
  2. Check System Settings → General → Login Items and remove anything you don’t recognise.
  3. Move unknown apps from Applications to the Trash, and remove unknown Safari or Chrome extensions.
  4. For a scan, use the free Malwarebytes for Mac.
🤖 Android
  1. Open the Play Store → your profile picture → Play Protect → Scan.
  2. If a bad app won’t uninstall, restart in safe mode (hold the power button, then hold Power off) and remove it there.
  3. Check Settings → Security → Device admin apps and switch off anything unknown.
  4. Only install apps from Google Play.
📱 iPhone and iPad
  1. Real viruses are very rare. Install the latest iOS update.
  2. Remove unknown profiles in Settings → General → VPN & Device Management.
  3. Calendar spam or pop-ups? Delete unknown calendar subscriptions and clear Settings → Apps → Safari → Clear History and Website Data.
🚩 Signs of an infection
  • Pop-ups or ads everywhere, even outside the browser.
  • A new home page, search engine or toolbar you didn’t choose.
  • Your antivirus was switched off without you doing it.
  • Friends get messages you didn’t send.
  • Your files have strange new names and a ransom note (ransomware).
🔑 After cleaning
  • Change your passwords from a different, clean device — email first, then banking.
  • Turn on two-step sign-in everywhere you can.
  • Check your bank account for payments you didn’t make.
  • Ransomware? Disconnect from the internet, don’t pay, and look for a free decryptor at nomoreransom.org.

What it checks

🎭 Disguises
Programs and scripts renamed to look like a PDF, photo or document; double extensions like invoice.pdf.exe; hidden right-to-left letters that flip the end of a name; shortcuts (.lnk) that run commands.
📄 Documents
Word, Excel and PowerPoint macros and the commands they run, templates loaded from the internet, embedded objects, old equation-editor exploits in RTF and .doc, and JavaScript, auto-run actions, launch commands and attached files in PDFs.
🗜️ Zip files
Opens zip files (and files inside them) to find scripts, shortcuts and programs, password-protected contents, and zip bombs.
📜 Scripts and web pages
Hidden PowerShell, download-and-run commands, attempts to switch off antivirus, ransomware commands, and HTML attachments that fake a login page or smuggle a download.
🔗 Links
Look-alike brand names (paypa1, apple.com.verify-id.top), hidden real addresses after an @, international look-alike letters, raw IP addresses, link shorteners and risky domain endings.
🔒 Private
Everything runs in this page with JavaScript. Files are never uploaded, and links are never opened. The VirusTotal buttons send only the fingerprint or the link, and only when you press them.
