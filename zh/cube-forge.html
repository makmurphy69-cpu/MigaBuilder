How Cube Forge works

Any size: the puzzle is a grid of small cubes with a sticker on every outside face. A move turns one layer (or several) a quarter or half turn; on a cuboid a layer whose face is not square can only turn halfway. The colours follow the usual scheme: white opposite yellow, green opposite blue and red opposite orange.

Solve with me uses the beginner layer-by-layer method, the way people learn it: a white cross, the white corners, the middle layer, then the yellow side in four short steps. It finds each piece, tells you where it goes and which algorithm to use. It is not the shortest solution (about 100–200 moves) but every move makes sense. On a 2×2 it does the same steps with only the corners.

Bigger cubes are solved by reduction: make the centres, pair up the edges, then solve it like a 3×3, with two extra algorithms for the parity cases — all in the Learn tab.

About Cube Forge

Cube Forge is a 3D Rubik’s-style cube trainer: choose a cube from 2×2 to 10×10 or a cuboid, turn it, learn the notation and the beginner method, and get a step-by-step solution for your own 3×3 or 2×2.

How to use Cube Forge

  1. Pick a puzzle or move the Layers slider, then turn it by dragging the stickers or with the move buttons.
  2. Open Learn and press “Show me” on each step of the beginner method to see it played on the cube.
  3. Scramble the cube or paint the colours of your own, press “Solve my cube”, and follow the solution move by move.

Can it solve a 4×4 or bigger?

It teaches the reduction method with the parity algorithms for big cubes, and ↶ Undo can take back every turn. The automatic step-by-step solver is for the 3×3 and the 2×2.

Is Cube Forge free?

Yes. It is free to use, with no account, no watermark and no paywall.

Are my files uploaded?

No. It runs entirely in your browser, so your cubes and times stay on your device.
