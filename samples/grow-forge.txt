1. TOMATO  🍅
   Sown/planted: 16 Nov 2026  (day 0: Waiting to sprout)
   Expect sprouts: 21 Nov 2026 – 26 Nov 2026
   First harvest: 14 Feb 2027 – 16 Mar 2027
   Light: Full sun · Water: medium · Temperature: 18–30 °C · Spacing: 45–60 cm (bush) or 60 cm (cordon)
   Steps:
   [ ] Sow 2–3 seeds per small pot or cell, 6 mm deep, in seed compost. Keep at 20–25 °C and moist, not wet.
   [ ] When seedlings show their first true leaves, keep the strongest one and give it as much light as possible so it does not get tall and floppy.
   [ ] Move to a 9 cm pot when it has 2–3 true leaves, burying the stem up to the first leaves — tomatoes grow extra roots along a buried stem.
   [ ] Harden off for 7–10 days: put it outside in the day and bring it in at night.
   [ ] Plant out after the last frost in full sun, deep again, with a stake or cage. Use a 20–30 litre pot or more per plant.
   [ ] Water deeply 2–3 times a week (daily in hot weather in pots), always at the soil, never on the leaves.
   [ ] Once the first flowers set fruit, feed every 1–2 weeks with a high-potash (tomato) feed.
   [ ] On cordon (tall) types, pinch out the side shoots that grow in the angle between stem and leaf. Leave bush types alone.
   [ ] Pick when fully coloured and slightly soft. Before the first frost, pick the green ones and ripen them indoors next to a banana.
   Care:
   - Water deeply and regularly — uneven watering causes split fruit and blossom end rot.
   - Feed with high-potash feed from first fruit.
   - Remove lower leaves touching the soil to slow disease.
   - Stop tall plants at 4–5 trusses of flowers in cool climates by pinching off the top, so the fruit has time to ripen.
   Watch for:
   - Black, sunken patch on the fruit bottom (blossom end rot): Irregular watering stops calcium reaching the fruit. Water evenly and mulch.
   - Brown blotches on leaves and stems, fruit rotting (late blight): Remove affected leaves at once, keep leaves dry, grow under cover or choose blight-resistant varieties.
   - Yellow lower leaves: Usually hunger or age. Feed, and remove old leaves.
   - Fruit splitting: Heavy watering after dry spells. Water little and often instead.
   - Whitefly or aphids: Spray with soapy water, hang yellow sticky traps, plant marigolds or basil nearby.
   - Flowers fall off: Too hot (over 32 °C) or too cold at night, or dry. Shade at midday and water well.
   Harvest: Pick when the fruit is fully coloured. Store at room temperature, not in the fridge, for the best taste.

General guidance — local conditions vary. Never eat a wild plant or mushroom unless you are sure what it is.
