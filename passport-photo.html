How to take a photo that gets accepted

  • Light: face a window or a lamp so the light is even, with no shadows on your face or behind you.
  • Background: stand about half a metre in front of a plain white or light wall.
  • Camera: at eye level, about 1–1.5 m away. Ask someone else to take it — selfies distort the face.
  • Expression: neutral, mouth closed, both eyes open and looking at the camera.
  • Head: straight, not tilted. Hair away from your eyes; the whole face and ears' outline visible if your country asks for it.
  • Glasses: take them off — the US, UK and many others no longer allow them.
  • Head coverings: only for religious or medical reasons, with the face fully visible from chin to forehead.
  • Recent: most countries need a photo taken in the last 6 months, unedited (no filters or beauty retouching).
  • Children and babies: lay a baby on a white sheet and photograph from above; eyes may be closed for newborns in most countries.
  • Check the rules: requirements change. The passport or visa office's own website always has the final word.
