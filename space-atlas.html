Space Atlas

The history of astronomy and space exploration: ancient sky-watchers, telescopes and discoveries, rockets and satellites, astronauts, the Moon landings, probes to every planet and the great space telescopes.

About Space Atlas

Space Atlas covers astronomy and spaceflight from ancient observatories and the ideas of Aristarchus, Aryabhata, Copernicus, Galileo, Kepler and Newton, through the discovery of planets, galaxies, pulsars, exoplanets and black holes, to the space age: rockets, satellites, the first animals and people in space, the Moon landings, space stations, rovers and probes to every planet, and the Hubble and James Webb telescopes — with missions from the USA, the Soviet Union and Russia, Europe, China, India and Japan.

How to use Space Atlas

  1. Search a mission, a planet, a telescope or an astronomer, or pick a group such as The Moon or Human spaceflight.
  2. Open a card to read what happened, how it worked and what we learned.
  3. Use the timeline to follow astronomy from ancient times to today.
  4. Play the quiz, or download a printable quiz with answers.

What does Space Atlas cover?

Ancient astronomy, great astronomers and discoveries, telescopes and observatories, rockets, satellites, human spaceflight, the Moon, probes to the planets, space stations and today’s missions.

Does it include missions from countries other than the USA?

Yes. It includes Soviet and Russian, European, Chinese, Indian and Japanese missions, as well as American ones.

Is Space Atlas free?

Yes. It is free to use, with no account and no paywall. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded.
