The classic lines plan: profile (side), half-breadth plan (top, one side) and body plan (cross-sections — bow half on the right, stern half on the left). The blue line is the waterline for your load.

The righting-arm (GZ) curve shows how hard the boat pushes back upright when it heels. Above zero it rights itself; where the curve crosses zero again it would capsize (angle of vanishing stability). It assumes the hull and deck are watertight and the load stays in place.

Offsets are the measurements a builder lofts full size: at each station, the height above the baseline and the half-breadth from the centreline, in millimetres.

Every weight and where it sits. The boat trims until the centre of buoyancy is right under the centre of gravity (LCG). Move crew and gear to level it.

Hydrostatics

    How Boat Forge works

    Shape: the hull is built from about 60 cross-sections. Length, beam and depth set the size; the shape sliders control how full the bow is, where the widest point sits, the transom width, the V of the bottom (deadrise), how much the bottom curves up at the ends (rocker), the rise of the deck line (sheer) and how far the sides lean out (flare).

    Floating: it adds up the weight of the hull (from its surface area and material), deck, engine, fuel, crew, gear, cabin, rig and ballast, then sinks the hull until it displaces that weight of water (Archimedes), and tilts it until it is balanced. From that it works out draft, freeboard, the waterline, form coefficients and the metacentric height (GM).

    Speed: planing hulls use Crouch’s formula; displacement hulls are limited by their hull speed (1.34 × √waterline length in feet, in knots). Sailboats get sail-area/displacement and displacement/length ratios. These are estimates for comparing designs — a real boat also needs a professional structural design and must meet your country’s rules (e.g. ISO 12217 stability, CE/RCD, USCG).

    Boat Forge is a design and learning tool. Check any boat you build with a qualified designer and test it in safe, shallow water with buoyancy aids.
