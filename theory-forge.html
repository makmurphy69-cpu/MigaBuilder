    How to get the most out of it

    🗺️ Follow the path

    The lessons are in an order that builds up: notes → rhythm → scales → intervals → chords → progressions → modes → writing your own. Each takes about 5 minutes. Pass the quiz to tick it off.

    🎸 Use it straight away

    After each lesson, open the song or jam it links to. Theory sticks when you hear it in music you can play. The instrument at the top lights up every note.

    👂 Ear training, little and often

    Five minutes of ear training a day beats an hour once a week. Tie each interval to a song you know — the list is in the intervals lesson.

    🎯 Practise with “wait for me”

    In a song, press Practice: the music waits on each note until you play it — on the screen, the computer keyboard (A W S E D F T G Y H U J K), a MIDI keyboard or your real instrument through the microphone.

    🐢 Slow it down

    Play songs at 50% or 75% speed with the click on, then speed up. Playing slowly and correctly is the fastest way to play fast.

    🔁 Improvise

    In Progressions & jam, loop the chords and play any of the lit notes. Start with 2 or 3 notes, leave space, and finish phrases on the root.

    About Theory Forge

    Theory Forge is a free music theory course and practice tool. It combines short lessons, an explorer for scales, chords and progressions, songs with sheet music and tab, ear training and playable instruments, so you can learn an idea, hear it and use it on your own instrument right away.

    How to use Theory Forge

    1. Pick your instrument at the top: piano, guitar, bass, ukulele, mandolin or violin. Click, tap or use your computer keyboard to play it.
    2. Open Learn and start with lesson 1. Each lesson has sounds to try, a “use it in real life” box and a short quiz.
    3. Open Songs to learn a tune with sheet music and tab, and press Practice so the song waits for you, or open Scales & chords to see any scale, chord or progression on your instrument.

    What is the best way to learn music theory?

    Learn one small idea at a time, hear it, and use it straight away on your instrument and in a real song. Theory Forge follows that order: a short lesson with sounds, a quick quiz, then a song or a jam that uses the idea. A few minutes of ear training every day helps more than long sessions once a week.

    Do I need an instrument to use Theory Forge?

    No. Every lesson, scale, chord and song can be played on the built-in piano, guitar, bass, ukulele, mandolin or violin, using the mouse, a touch screen or your computer keyboard. If you have a real instrument, the practice mode can listen through the microphone, and a MIDI keyboard works too.

    Can I read tabs and sheet music here?

    Yes. Every song shows the sheet music and the tab together, with the note you are on lit up. Lesson 5 explains how to read tab, and you can download the tab of a song, scale or progression as a text file.

    Is Theory Forge free?

    Yes. It is free with no account and no ads. All sounds are made in your browser, and your progress is saved on your device.
